1. Never trust a backtest that invents the ticks. A tester that fills each minute with smooth generated ticks (MetaTrader's "Every tick" mode without real ticks, or any "1-minute OHLC" style test of a rule this small) hands these rules money that does not exist: the proof section shows it on a random price. In MetaTrader 5 use only "Every tick based on real ticks" on your broker's own XAUUSD history, with your real spread and commission.
  2. Treat the spread as the opponent. With no edge, every rule here earns about $0 before costs and loses the cost of every trade after. A raw-spread / ECN account (gold from about $0.10 spread plus about $7 per lot commission) is the minimum. Fewer, larger trades lose less to costs than many tiny ones.
  3. Size from "Balance needed", not from the win rate. Use the Lot × and balance boxes: a row is only usable when its chip says survives with room to spare (at least twice the balance needed). Martingale and recovery rows win most days and still blow accounts; their worst day and their biggest position are the numbers that matter.
  4. Forward-test on demo for 4 weeks with the exact EA, lot and daily stop, and compare each day with this lab's average day and worst day. A live result well below the test means slippage, rejected orders or a wider spread than modelled.
  5. Only then go live small, with the daily loss limit hard-coded in the EA (not only in your head), on an account where one worst day is under a quarter of the balance, at a broker that allows hedging, scalping and EAs.

Data: MGC (micro gold) and MNQ (micro Nasdaq) 1-minute futures bars, New York time, standing in for XAUUSD and NAS100; futures and CFD prices move together, but CFD spread, swap and fills differ. The lab acts on each minute's real open and close, so a rule here trades at most about twice a minute, fewer times than the same rule on live ticks; on a price with no edge that changes the number of trades (and so the costs), not the result before costs. Every day starts flat and closes everything at the session's end (no overnight swap). Historical study, not a trading signal.
