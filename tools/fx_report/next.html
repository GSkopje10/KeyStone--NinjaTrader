1. Re-run the top rows on real ticks before risking a cent. These results walk four prices per minute (open, high, low, close). With a $1 target on gold, many trades open and close inside one minute, so the order of high and low decides them. In MetaTrader 5's Strategy Tester choose "Every tick based on real ticks" on your broker's XAUUSD, with your real spread and commission. Keep only what still makes money there. The "Adverse path" column shows how much each row depends on that order.
  2. Get the cost per trade as low as possible. For any high-frequency rule the cost per round trip is the whole game: it is charged on every trade, wins and losses alike. A raw-spread / ECN account (spread from about $0.10 on gold plus about $7 per lot commission) is the minimum. Check that your broker allows hedging, scalping and EAs, and has no minimum holding time.
  3. Size from "Balance needed", not from the win rate. Use the Lot × and balance boxes above: a row is only usable when its "At your balance" chip says survives with room to spare (at least twice the balance needed). Martingale and recovery rows win most days and still blow accounts; their worst day is the number that matters.
  4. Forward-test on demo for 4 weeks with the exact EA, lot and daily stop, and compare each day with this lab's average day and worst day. A live result far below the test means slippage, rejected orders or a spread wider than modelled.
  5. Then go live small, with the daily loss limit hard-coded in the EA (not only in your head), and the account sized so one worst day is under a quarter of it.

Data: MGC (micro gold) and MNQ (micro Nasdaq) 1-minute futures bars, New York time, standing in for XAUUSD and NAS100. Futures and CFD prices move together within cents, but the CFD spread, swap and broker fills differ. Every day starts flat and closes everything at the session's end (no overnight swap). This is a historical study, not a trading signal.
