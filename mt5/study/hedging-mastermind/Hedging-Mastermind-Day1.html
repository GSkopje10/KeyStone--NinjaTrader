What he does — candidate rules

  1. One fixed size: every leg is 10 lots.No martingale and no growing lead — all 20 legs the same size. Scaled: 0.01 lot per ≈ $1,060 of balance.
  2. A grid about 2 points apart.The next leg is usually 1.6–3.0 from the last (median ≈ 2.3); bigger gaps only after a fast move.
  3. Hedges are placed in advance as stop orders, about 2–5 points beyond the last leg.Several filled during fast moves, with 0.5–0.9 points of slippage.
  4. He moves the hedge stop toward the price when the basket is winning.17:35: SELL STOP 3923.95 moved up to 3927.96 as the price rose — the same idea as SpasiX's ⚡ QUICK HEDGE.
  5. Stops are removed when the price runs away from them, and all of them when he closes a basket.15:00 (21 pts away), 15:57 (4.7 away), 17:33 limit (3.7 away); 16:16 the buy stop was pulled with the floating back near flat (−$516) and the basket was closed minutes later; 17:39 both stops went with the basket.
  6. The whole basket closes at once at about +$1,600–3,500 (0.15–0.35% of the balance).The deep morning basket was closed at only +$1,788 after 7½ hours — getting out was the goal there.
  7. He accepts a floating loss of up to ≈ 3.5% of the balance.On $5k that would be ≈ −$175 at the scaled size.

Day 1 only — each rule is a candidate until more days confirm it.

Draft automation — backtest of his day-1 rules

The rules above as an automation, on a $5,000 account: a buy stop and a sell stop around the price to start, then a new BUY only above the basket's highest leg + step and a new SELL only below its lowest leg − step; every leg the same size (0.01 lot per $1,060 of balance, his ratio); the whole basket closes at the target; spread 0.25, commission 0.001% per side, 1:500, the broker's stop-out at a 50% margin level.

What it means: on the day he streamed, his rules work. Over a whole year they are not enough on their own — with no limit the ladder piles up hundreds of legs through long trends and the account is stopped out within weeks. A 10-leg limit keeps most runs alive, but the profit is thin and baskets can stay stuck for months. So day 1 shows how he manages a basket, not yet when he starts one and when he stops adding — that is what the next videos (and the candles for his moving averages) have to show. Not something to run on a real account yet.
