2 · Divided rotation: several smaller trades a day

Dividing the day passes fewer accounts. The best divided plan (+$1,500 / −$750 per trade, several tries a day) reaches about 27–28%, against about 31% for one full trade a day. Small brackets are much worse: +$300 / −$300 passes 3–8%, and +$500 / −$500 at most 17%, because each extra trade pays costs and a small stop is hit by ordinary noise before the target.

3 · Funded trailing drawdown (TPT PRO 50K)

Intraday trailing $2,000: the floor follows the highest balance including open profit until it reaches the start balance ($50,000), then stops (the "lock"). Payout-ready = closed balance ≥ $52,250 (the $2,000 buffer plus the $250 minimum payout). Every 3rd trading day since July 2020 starts a new account, followed for up to 60 sessions. 4,653 plans tested.

Why letting profit run hurts here: every dollar of open profit raises the floor. A trade at +$1,500 that comes back to 0 has moved the floor from $48,000 to $49,500, so the account lost $1,500 of room without losing a dollar. With intraday trailing, a target taken as a limit is the only profit that "counts" without raising the floor first. The same plans under end-of-day trailing pass 3–9 points more.
Best plan: random 9:30 · MNQ 10 · +$2,250 / ride to floor · fixed · 1/day. Payout-ready in 38.2% of accounts (2020–23 37.5%, 2024–26 39.1%), usually on the first day. The rest blow on that trade.
  1. The math says about a third whatever you do. Reaching +$2,250 before falling $2,000 from the highest point happens about exp(−2,250 / 2,000) ≈ 32% of the time with no edge. Nasdaq's rise adds a few points.
  2. The slow, small plans (MNQ 2–4, +$750–1,000 targets) end at the same ~36–37% after 9–19 sessions, with more exposure to news, rule breaks and fees. The one-shot answers the question in a day.
  3. TPT funded allows no automation: you click. The 11-second rule is met by any trade closed after its entry minute. Every simulated trade is held at least a minute. In live automation, keep a minimum hold of 11 s for more than half of the trades (a parameter in the lab).

5 funded accounts: rotation vs all at once vs one at a time

Accounts payout-ready out of 5 (within 60 sessions and within 20), how often at least 3 of 5 get there, how often none does, and the sessions until all 5 are resolved. One account at a time (the next starts when the previous is payout-ready or blown) gives about 1.8 of 5 with the one-shot, and none of 5 only 10–16% of the time. "All at once, own side" averages a little more (about 1.9), but it holds opposite positions inside one firm at the same moment (hedging, banned by most firms). "All at once, same side" is all-or-nothing: 62% of the time none of 5. Rotation through the day lowers the one-shot to about 1.5 of 5, because the later accounts enter later with less of the day left.

The indicators to build (one line each in the studio):
  1. EVAL: "+$1,500 before the floor: about 54% at 2:00, 51% at 9:30". Trade at 2:00; after 13:30 the chance drops (46%) and many trades are still open at the close.

  3. FUNDED · push: "+$1,500 before −$500 with the London side: 17% target, many closed at the session end". It's the payout engine: small risk, large target. Expect most days to end small.
  4. PAYOUT DAY: when the 5th qualifying day is reached, show the payout now (50% of profit, capped, 90% to you). Then show the room left after the withdrawal; protect it with a qualify-size day when the room is under $1,000.
