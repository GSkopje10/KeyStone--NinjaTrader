The YouTube method, tested

Never stop out: flip at the edge of the consolidation, wait inside it

Rules from the transcript: stop hedging inside a consolidation and hedge again only at its high or low. Here the consolidation is the high and low of the last K hours. When price breaks the box against the basket, 2 units open the other way, so the net position flips to the breakout side and stays one unit. Inside the box nothing happens. The basket closes as soon as everything together is up by the target (in H1 ATR). Positions are held overnight, so swap matters. Dollars are for 0.1 lot gold ($10 a point) and 2 lots NAS100 ($2 a point).

Math

The numbers behind every rule

Expectancy decides everything

E[R] = WR × (T/S − k) − (1 − WR) × (1 + k)

T = target, S = stop, k = cost per trade as a share of the stop. A small target needs a very high win rate, and costs weigh most on small stops, which is why 1-minute scalps rarely survive.

A hedge does not change the odds

Price has no memory of your basket. Opening 2× opposite at the stop line is the same bet as closing the trade and opening 1× the other way, plus extra spread. Zone recovery wins small and often, then loses a lot at once: a martingale. It only helps if the entry already has an edge, and even then the tail grows.

P(basket hits −2.5R) ≈ the chance price crosses the zone 2–3 times before the target

Sizing: fractional Kelly

f* = E[R] / Var[R]  → risk ¼ f* per trade
